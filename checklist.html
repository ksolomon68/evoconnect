Vendor Readiness Checklist

Know Exactly What You Need

Three tracks walk you from zero to federal subcontractor. Check off items as you complete them — your progress saves in your browser.

Track A — Federal Registration

SAM.gov Registration

SAM.gov (System for Award Management) is the federal government's official contractor database. You must be registered to receive federal contract payments. The process is free and takes 2–3 weeks.

0 of 7 steps complete

Get a Unique Entity Identifier (UEI)

Register on SAM.gov to receive your free UEI number. This replaced the old DUNS number system in 2022.

Time: ~30 minutes | Cost: Free
Gather Required Business Information

You will need: legal business name, physical address, business structure (sole prop, LLC, etc.), EIN or SSN, and bank account info for electronic funds transfer.

Time: ~1 hour | Cost: Free
Complete the SAM.gov Registration Form

Fill out all sections on SAM.gov including your NAICS codes (use the codes WorkForce Connect provided after you registered), business size, and representations & certifications.

Time: 2–3 hours | Cost: Free
Submit and Wait for Activation

SAM.gov typically takes 2–3 weeks to activate new registrations. You will receive a confirmation email when your registration is active.

Time: 2–3 weeks processing | Cost: Free
Set Up Electronic Funds Transfer (EFT)

During SAM.gov registration, add your bank account information for direct deposit of contract payments. This is required for federal payments.

Time: 15 minutes | Cost: Free
Search USASpending.gov for Opportunities

Once registered, search USASpending.gov and SAM.gov Opportunities using your NAICS codes to find active subcontracting opportunities.

Ongoing activity
Renew SAM.gov Registration Annually

SAM.gov registrations must be renewed every 12 months or they expire. Set a calendar reminder to avoid losing your active status.

Annual requirement | Cost: Free
Track B — Certifications

State & Federal Certifications

Certifications unlock preferential treatment in government contracting. Each certification has eligibility requirements — check which ones apply to you and your business.

0 of 6 steps complete

Determine Which Certifications Apply to You

Review eligibility for: HUBZone (location-based), WOSB (women-owned), SDVOSB (service-disabled veteran), and SBA 8(a) (socially/economically disadvantaged). You can hold multiple certifications.

Time: ~1 hour research
Apply for HUBZone (if your address qualifies)

HUBZone (Historically Underutilized Business Zone) gives a 10% price evaluation preference on federal contracts. Check if your business address qualifies at SBA HUBZone Map.

Time: 60–90 days | Cost: Free
Apply for WOSB Certification (women-owned businesses)

Woman-Owned Small Business certification provides access to WOSB set-aside contracts. Apply through the SBA Certify portal.

Time: 30–90 days | Cost: Free
Apply for SDVOSB Certification (veterans)

Service-Disabled Veteran-Owned Small Business certification opens VA set-aside contracts and preference programs. Apply through the SBA Veteran portal.

Time: 30–60 days | Cost: Free
Apply for SBA 8(a) Business Development (if eligible)

The SBA 8(a) program is a 9-year business development program for socially and economically disadvantaged individuals. Provides access to sole-source and set-aside contracts. Apply at SBA Certify.

Time: 90+ days | Cost: Free
Register with Texas HUB Program (if Texas-based)

Texas-based businesses can register as a Historically Underutilized Business (HUB) with the Texas Comptroller for state contracting preferences. Register at Texas HUB Program.

Time: 30–60 days | Cost: Free
Track C — Sub-Ready Documents

Subcontractor Readiness

Prime contractors need to verify your qualifications before they can hire you on a federal contract. These documents prove you are ready to work.

0 of 7 steps complete

Form a Legal Business Entity

Register a sole proprietorship, LLC, or corporation with your state. An LLC provides liability protection and is the most common structure for trade contractors. Cost varies by state ($50–$500).

Time: 1–2 weeks | Cost: ~$100–$300
Get an EIN (Employer Identification Number)

Apply for a free EIN from the IRS at IRS.gov. Required for SAM.gov registration and separating personal from business finances.

Time: 15 minutes | Cost: Free
Open a Dedicated Business Bank Account

A separate business checking account is required for receiving federal contract payments and demonstrates financial separation from personal funds. Most banks offer free business accounts.

Time: 1 day | Cost: Free–$15/month
Write a One-Page Capability Statement

A capability statement is a one-page document that summarizes your trade expertise, past projects, certifications, NAICS codes, and contact information. It is the primary document prime contractors use to evaluate subcontractors.

Time: 2–4 hours | Cost: Free (templates available online)
Get a Certificate of Insurance (COI)

Most federal contracts require general liability insurance (minimum $1M per occurrence). Request a COI from your insurance provider. Workers' comp may also be required depending on the project.

Time: 1–3 days | Cost: $500–$2,000/year
Obtain Relevant Trade Licenses

Many trades require state or local licenses (electrical, plumbing, HVAC, contractor). Verify your state's licensing requirements and get licensed before pursuing federal work in those trades.

Time: Varies by trade and state
Complete Your WorkForce Connect Profile

Make sure your WorkForce Connect profile is complete with accurate trade category, certifications, experience summary, and location. Prime contractors search and filter by these fields when looking for subcontractors.

Time: 15 minutes | Cost: Free
